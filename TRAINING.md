# Training the staff

How to teach each person their part, on a real tablet, without touching a real
figure.

Nobody needs to understand the system. They need to know their own four or five
taps, and what to do on the day something is odd. This is built so each person
learns only their own job.

For how it all fits together, read [HANDBOOK.md](HANDBOOK.md) yourself first.

---

## Before anybody sits down

**Set up a practice tablet.**

1. Sign in as the owner on the tablet you are going to train on.
2. Dashboard → scroll to the bottom → **Start practice on this tablet**.
3. Read the confirmation, then **Start practice**.
4. The tablet reloads with a banner across the top. That banner must stay there
   the whole session. If it is gone, stop — you are on a real till.
5. Sign out. Hand the tablet over.

**What this means.** Everything the trainee does is real to them and invisible to
the business. They cannot break anything, cannot lose a real sale, and cannot
put a wrong figure in front of the owner. Let them make mistakes — the mistakes
are the training.

**When you are done**, tap **End practice** on the banner. If you forget, the
tablet puts itself back to normal by the next morning on its own.

> Train on the shop's own tablet, not a spare. Muscle memory is for the tablet
> that will actually be at that counter.

## The cashier — about 40 minutes

The one who takes money. Most of the business runs on this person doing four
things well.

### 1 · Signing in (2 min)

Tap your name, type your four-digit PIN.

**Your PIN is yours.** Not written on the till, not on a sticker, not in the
group chat. It goes on every sale you ring, and it is how the owner knows a
correction was yours and not someone else's.

Sign in once at the start of your shift. **Do not sign out during the day** —
signing back in needs the internet, and if the wifi is down at that moment you
cannot sell at all.

### 2 · Ringing a sale (15 min — spend the time here)

Practise until it is boring:

- **By code.** Type `01`, then the quantity. The codes are the same numbers on
  the sheet by the till — the ones you already know.
- **By name.** Type `bread` and pick from what comes up.
- **A quantity.** Type `12`, do not tap `+` twelve times.
- **Something weighed.** Type `4.5`, or `450g`. The system works out the price.
- **A mistake in the basket.** Remove the line and ring it again.
- **Clear the whole bill.** It asks first if there is more than one line.

Then the payment:

- **Cash** — type what the customer handed over. The change is on screen. Read
  it out loud; that is the habit that catches a mis-key.
- **Card** — just tap it.
- **Print the receipt.**

Have them ring twenty sales. Not five. Twenty is where it stops being a screen
and starts being a reflex.

### 3 · The two corrections (10 min)

Both leave a permanent record with their name on it. **That is not a trap — it
is the reason they are allowed to do it at all without asking anyone.**

- **Fix** — rang it as cash, customer paid by card. One tap.
- **Void** — the whole sale was wrong. Pick a reason. The sale stays on the
  list, greyed out, so the day still adds up.

Say this out loud: *"You will never be in trouble for a void with a reason on
it. You will be in trouble for a drawer that does not match and no void."*

### 4 · When the internet is down (5 min)

The single most important thing this person can know.

Turn the tablet's wifi off in front of them, then have them ring three sales.
It works. There is a small note on screen saying the tablet is offline.

Then say:

- **Keep selling. Nothing is lost.** The sales go up on their own when the wifi
  comes back.
- **Do not re-ring a sale** because the screen looked slow.
- **Do not sign out.**
- **Never clear the app's data**, and if the app ever says it cannot start, tap
  **Try again** — not Reset.

Turn the wifi back on and let them watch the sales go up.

### 5 · Closing the day (10 min)

Do a whole close, twice.

1. **Count the cash.** Type what is actually in the drawer. If it disagrees, it
   says so. *Type what is really there anyway.* A short drawer that is recorded
   honestly is a small problem; a drawer made to match is a big one.
2. **Count the shelf.** Item by item: how many left, and of those how many are
   going in the bin, staying for tomorrow, or going back to the bakery.
3. **Tomorrow's order.** Already filled in from what this shop actually sells on
   this weekday. Change any line. **Send it.** The day will not finish until
   this is sent — no order, no bread.
4. **Review and finish.**

Then show them the mistake: close the day, then **reopen** it. They can do that
themselves, any time, without asking the owner. It gets noted, and that is all.

### The card for the counter

> **The till**
> - Sign in at the start of your shift. Do not sign out during the day.
> - No internet? **Keep selling.** Nothing is lost. Do not ring anything twice.
> - Wrong payment type → **Fix**. Wrong sale → **Void**, pick a reason.
> - Count the drawer honestly at close. Type what is really there.
> - **Send tomorrow's order** before you finish the day.
> - Never clear the app's data. If it will not start, tap **Try again**.
> - Nothing is ever deleted, so nothing you do can be a disaster.

## The kitchen — about 20 minutes

### The morning list

Open **Bake**. One list, for all three shops, ready by 05:00.

For each line, record what actually came out of the oven — the number that came
out, not the number that was asked for.

- **A short bake is fine.** Record the real number and carry on; the list can
  still be finished. The vans have to leave.
- **A tray nobody ordered** goes under *Also baked today*. It is kept separate
  on purpose, so next week's order is not thrown off by a one-off.

Practise: work a full list, make one line deliberately short, add one extra.

### Loading the vans

Open **Dispatch**. One note per shop, already filled in. Adjust anything that is
actually different, then send.

When something comes back from a shop, count what actually came back rather than
accepting the number on the note.

### If there is no list at 05:15

**Phone whoever looks after the system.** Nobody in the building can create the
baking list by hand — the system will not allow it. This is the one problem the
kitchen cannot work around, and every minute counts.

### The card for the kitchen

> **The bake**
> - The list is ready by 05:00. If it is not there by 05:15, **phone [name]**.
> - Record what actually came out, not what was asked for.
> - Short is fine. Record it honestly and carry on.
> - Extra trays go under **Also baked today**.
> - Dispatch: check each note, adjust, send.
> - Returns: count what actually came back.

## The shop, taking a delivery — 10 minutes

Whoever meets the van.

Open **Stock**. The note is already on screen with what was sent.

- **Count what actually arrived.** Not what the paper says.
- Anything that does not match needs a **reason** from the list — damaged,
  short at the hub, and so on.
- Both numbers are kept, side by side, for good.

Say this clearly: **a short delivery is never counted as your waste.** That is
exactly why the reason is asked for. A shop that quietly accepts a short
delivery to avoid a fuss ends up looking like the shop that throws bread away.

Practise: accept one delivery clean, and one two items short with a reason.

## The owner — about 30 minutes

Not how to use the app. How to read it.

### Every morning, one screen

Open the dashboard. It opens with **your daily sheet** — the same line you write
on the pad, filled in by itself. Then **Needs a look**. If that list is empty,
the business ran yesterday.

### What each figure actually means

- **Total sale** is what was rung up, less voids and refunds. Not what is in the
  drawers — that is the closes.
- **Over / short** is drawer against rung-up. Small and both directions is
  normal. One shop always short in the same direction is a conversation.
- **Total stale** only appears once a shop has actually closed. Before that it
  says so rather than showing zero, because zero is a claim.
- **Distribution minus total sale** is not missing money. It is bread on a
  shelf. Stock on hand has the item-by-item version.
- **Contribution** per shop is not profit. Flour is bought once at the bakery
  and there is no honest way to split a sack, so only the whole business gets a
  profit figure. The screen says this on it.

### The two habits worth building

1. **Check the daily sheet against what the shops tell you**, every day, for the
   first fortnight. That is when a wrong price or a mis-set rate shows up, and
   all of it is cheap to fix on day two and expensive on day thirty.
2. **Read the Faults card.** If something broke on a tablet, it is there. A
   cashier will not always tell you.

### What you can do that nobody else can

- Change prices and the catalogue.
- Set this morning's rates for anything priced daily.
- Add and remove staff — **tell whoever maintains the system when you add
  someone**, because their permissions need a second step until the server jobs
  are deployed.
- See the money screens.
- Turn a tablet into a practice tablet.

## Rolling it out

Run the shops on paper and on the system at the same time for **two weeks**.
Not one. The second week is where the odd cases turn up — the returned item, the
short delivery, the day somebody forgets to close.

Each evening, compare the daily sheet on the screen with the one on the pad. If
they disagree, find out why *that evening*, while everyone still remembers.

Order to train in:

1. **The kitchen**, a day before anyone else. Nothing else works without a
   baking list.
2. **One cashier per shop** — the most confident one. They teach the rest.
3. **Everyone else**, taught by their own colleague, not by you. Someone who
   learned it yesterday explains it better than someone who built it.

Stop the paper only when a full week has gone by with no disagreement.

## The three things that actually go wrong

Worth saying to everybody, whatever their job.

1. **Somebody clears the app's data** to "fix" a slow tablet, and an afternoon's
   unsent sales go with it. → *Never clear it. Tap Try again.*
2. **Somebody re-rings a sale** because the screen looked slow with the wifi
   down. Now the day is over by Rs 900 and nobody can say which sale was the
   double. → *It saved. It always saves.*
3. **A shop closes without sending tomorrow's order**, and gets no bread. → The
   system will not let them finish the day without it, but they can walk away
   mid-close. *Finish the close.*
