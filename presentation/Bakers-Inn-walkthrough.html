A day at Baker's Inn

One bakery, two shops, and everything that has to happen between them — from the order sent at closing time to the figure you write on the pad.

One bakery. Two shops.

Susan Road buys the flour, bakes, and sends bread out. The other two only sell. The system is built in exactly that shape.

When the baker starts

Three orders become one baking list

One tap in the kitchen adds up what the three shops sent last night. One list for all of them — not three to reconcile, and no clock to wait for.

A shop that did not order gets its last same weekday repeated, and the list says so.

Weighed items too. Loose biscuits are typed as 4.5, or 450g — the price follows.

Something you don't bake? A bottle of Coke goes on the bill with its own price, so the drawer still matches at close.

Paid by phone? JazzCash, Easypaisa and bank transfers are rung like anything else, with the transaction id kept against the sale. Only cash counts against the drawer at closing.

No internet, no problem. The tablet keeps every sale and sends them the moment the connection comes back. Nothing is lost and nothing is rung twice.

Then the shelf: what is left, and of that what goes in the bin, what stays for tomorrow, and what comes back to you.

Anyone can cancel a sale or reopen a day without asking you. Every one of them carries a name and reaches your screen the same day.
